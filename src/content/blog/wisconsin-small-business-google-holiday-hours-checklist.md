Wisconsin businesses should confirm Thanksgiving, Christmas, and New Year’s hours on Google in October—then verify every customer-facing channel again two weeks before each holiday. Updating Google Business Profile is necessary, but it is not the whole job. Your website, phone greeting, booking calendar, ads, chat, staff schedule, and emergency-routing rules should all give the same answer.

The timing is supported by recurring search behavior. In the [Google Trends view for “holiday hours” in Wisconsin over the past five years](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?date=today%205-y&geo=US-WI&q=holiday%20hours), accessed September 27, 2026, interest rose around Thanksgiving and climbed more sharply during Christmas week in every completed holiday season shown. The weekly index reached 66 for December 19, 2021; 52 for December 18, 2022; 56 for December 24, 2023; 88 for December 22, 2024; and the five-year relative peak of 100 for December 21, 2025. Thanksgiving-week readings also rose above nearby fall weeks, including 18, 19, 20, 20, and 25 in 2021 through 2025. These are normalized Google Trends values—not search-volume counts or projected leads—but the repeated pattern makes late September and October the practical preparation window.

## The short answer: use a three-pass holiday-hours process

| When | What to do | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early October | Approve hours, closures, on-call rules, and booking capacity for each holiday | Operations |
| Two weeks before each holiday | Update Google special hours and every customer-facing channel | Marketing + operations |
| 48 hours before and the first business day after | Verify what customers see, then restore normal routing and notices | Named staff member |

Google specifically recommends confirming [special hours for official holidays even when they match regular hours](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6303076?hl=en). That confirmation tells customers the displayed schedule was considered rather than merely inherited.

## Start with the operating decision, not the profile edit

Before anyone opens Google Business Profile, decide what the business can actually support. A home-service company may close the office but keep an on-call technician. A showroom may accept online requests while its doors are locked. A restaurant, clinic, or retail location may have one set of customer-facing hours and another schedule for pickup or specific services.

Write down, for each date:

- Whether the business is open, closed, or operating reduced hours.
- Whether phones are answered live, sent to voicemail, or routed to an after-hours team.
- Whether web forms, chat, and online booking remain available.
- Which services are available and which are paused.
- The earliest honest callback or appointment window.
- Who has authority to change the public message if staffing changes.

This prevents the most common failure: a correct Google listing attached to an incorrect phone greeting or booking calendar.

## Use the right Google hours field

Google distinguishes regular hours, special hours, and longer closures. Its [special-hours instructions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6303076?hl=en) say special hours are appropriate for temporary changes or closures lasting up to six consecutive days. Google’s [closure guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/15300196?hl=en) says to use “Temporarily closed” for a closure longer than seven days, an off-season closure, or an indefinite closure.

For a normal Thanksgiving Day closure or reduced Christmas Eve schedule, use special hours. Do not change regular weekly hours just to cover one date. Do not mark a business temporarily closed for a one-day holiday. If the business is normally open 24 hours but closes for part of a holiday, enter the actual special-hour window instead of assuming the 24-hour label will explain the exception.

Google’s own workflow is straightforward: open the verified profile, choose **Edit profile**, open **Hours**, edit **Special hours**, select the date, enter the hours or mark it closed, and save. The operational work around that edit is where most small teams need a checklist.

## Keep the website and Google answer consistent

Customers may reach the business through a branded search, a service page, a local landing page, an ad, or a saved bookmark. Put a concise holiday notice where it helps them act—not as a pop-up that blocks the entire site.

A useful notice states:

1. The affected date.
2. The customer-facing hours or closure.
3. Whether calls, forms, or emergency requests are monitored.
4. When normal service resumes.
5. The next accurate action: call, book, leave a request, or return later.

For service-area businesses, confirm that the notice and the [service-area pages](/areas/) do not imply availability in places or time windows the team cannot cover. If a holiday landing page is unnecessary, update the existing contact and booking paths instead of publishing a thin page that will be abandoned in January.

The same principle applies to your [Google Business Profile optimization](/blog/optimize-google-business-profile-southeastern-wisconsin/): accurate hours support the complete, trustworthy business information Google asks owners to maintain. They are not a seasonal keyword opportunity. Google’s representation guidelines require businesses to [use their real-world name and put hours in the designated business-information fields](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en), so do not append “Open Thanksgiving” or “24/7 Christmas Service” to the business name.

## Audit the channels customers use after Google

Once the profile is updated, test the entire path as if you were a customer.

### Phone and voicemail

Call from a number outside the company. Confirm the greeting names the right holiday, gives the correct reopening time, and explains whether messages are monitored. If an [AI call-handling workflow](/services/ai-call-handling/) answers after hours, its knowledge and escalation rules need the same schedule. It should not promise an arrival time, invent availability, or route a routine request as an emergency.

### Forms, chat, and booking

Submit a test request. Verify the confirmation page and email set an honest response expectation. Block unavailable appointment slots. If chat can qualify requests, tell it which services and counties remain active during the holiday window.

### Ads and campaign links

Pause or revise ads that promise same-day service when the relevant staff will be away. A paid click should not land on an outdated “open now” message. For campaigns that remain active, send customers to a page with the current schedule and usable next step.

### Social and Google posts

A Google Business Profile update can reinforce the schedule, but it should not substitute for special hours. Google says posts can share [announcements, updates, offers, and event information](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en). Use a short post to explain service availability, then keep the profile’s hours field authoritative.

## Build one source of truth for every holiday

A small spreadsheet can work if one person owns it. A growing business may benefit from a simple automation that pushes approved hours into staff tasks, website notices, phone scripts, and scheduled reminders.

At minimum, track:

| Field | Example decision |
| --- | --- |
| Date and holiday | Thursday, November 26 — Thanksgiving |
| Public status | Closed |
| Phone status | Voicemail; urgent clients use the established on-call number |
| Web requests | Accepted; reply begins Friday at 8 a.m. |
| Booking | Friday morning blocked for triage |
| Google owner | Operations manager |
| Verification deadline | Tuesday before the holiday |
| Reversion owner | Friday opening shift |

This is a good use case for [workflow and business automation](/services/workflow-business-automation/): the approved schedule can create channel-specific tasks and reminders without allowing an unreviewed system to publish operational claims. Keep a person responsible for final verification.

## Plan for changes after the schedule is published

Weather, illness, and call volume can change a Wisconsin holiday schedule quickly. Decide in advance which facts trigger an update and who makes it.

Examples include:

- An on-call technician becomes unavailable.
- A snow or ice event delays openings.
- The booking calendar fills beyond the stated window.
- A phone vendor or chatbot fails over to the wrong route.
- A location closes early while another location remains open.

Update the highest-intent surfaces first: Google hours, website notice, phone routing, and booking availability. Then update active ads and social posts. Record the time of the change so staff know which message is current.

## Do not automate holiday-hour claims without review

Automation is useful for reminders, synchronized drafts, and verification tasks. It is risky when it guesses whether a location is open or copies one location’s schedule to every profile.

Use a human approval step when:

- Locations have different staffing or local rules.
- Emergency service differs from office hours.
- A third-party answering service uses separate scripts.
- The business has departments with distinct customer-facing hours.
- A closure may extend beyond the period appropriate for special hours.

This is the same human-in-the-loop standard Badger Automation Group recommends for [AI used in local SEO](/blog/ai-local-human-in-the-loop/): software can prepare and check the work, but an accountable person should approve factual claims customers rely on.

## A 15-minute verification run

Two days before each holiday, assign one person to complete this check:

1. Search the business name on Google and confirm the displayed special hours.
2. Open the website on a phone and follow the primary call or booking action.
3. Call the public number from outside the company.
4. Submit a test form or chat request and read the confirmation message.
5. Check available appointment slots and automated follow-up timing.
6. Review active ads and scheduled posts for conflicting claims.
7. Confirm who will restore normal messaging after the holiday.

Take screenshots or log the result. If a customer later reports conflicting information, the record makes it easier to find which channel failed.

## Measure the operational result

Do not judge the work by profile views alone. After the holiday, review:

- Calls, forms, and bookings received while the office was closed.
- Requests that received an incorrect expectation or duplicate reply.
- Missed calls that did not enter a follow-up queue.
- Appointments booked into unavailable times.
- Staff time spent correcting inconsistent messages.
- Customer comments or reviews that mention hours or responsiveness.

If the process worked, save the checklist for the next holiday rather than rebuilding it from memory. If it failed, fix the source of truth and ownership before adding more automation.

October is early enough to make the operating decisions and close enough to Thanksgiving for the schedule to be real. By the time Wisconsin searches for holiday hours begin their annual rise, customers should find one clear answer everywhere they look.
